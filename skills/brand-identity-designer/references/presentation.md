# Presenting, feedback, scope and studio practice

Template for the checkpoint board: `assets/route-board.html` (copy it into the brand folder, fill every bracketed placeholder, delete the HTML comments, render with `scripts/render.py shot`). Evidence tags: `[practice]` studio doctrine, `[limited]` weak evidence.

## Territories and moodboards (before routes)
- Use when the client's taste is unknown, the budget allows an extra sign-off step, or stakeholders disagree about the feeling. Skip for lean packages and for refreshes with a clear platform; go straight to routes.
- 2 to 3 territories, one page each. A board holds: a 2 to 3 word name, the ranked traits it expresses, 9 to 15 images (mood, light, materials, type in use), 2 to 3 type samples, 3 to 5 swatches.
- Never fill a board with other companies' logos or characters: it invites "make it like that one". Translate a reference into traits ("calm, confident, monochrome").
- Ask the client to pick one territory or name what they would take from each, in terms of the platform. A territory is a feeling; a route is a designed answer.
- Partner, payment and certification badges come from the owner's official files; never redraw them. Use a labelled placeholder.

## Route board layout
Per route, in this order (the idea first, the evidence after):
| Block | Content |
|---|---|
| Header | route name, "recommended" badge on one route only |
| Mechanism | one sentence naming the visual idea behind mark, type and palette together |
| Mark | large, then at 32 and 16 px, one colour and reversed |
| Lockup | wordmark and symbol arrangement, plus avatar circle |
| Type | specimen with the real brand name and a real sentence from the client's world |
| Palette | 3 to 5 swatches with role names, hex, contrast ratio against the text colour used on it |
| Touchpoint | the primary touchpoint, then 3 to 5 more, then one hard case (busy photo, one-colour invoice, embroidered cap) |
| Checks and risks | measured results (contrast, 16 px, competitor distance, licences) and one risk line |
- One device per route, 4 to 6 touchpoints at the same finish. Background patterns sit on the spacing scale (pitch 16, 24, 32 or 48 px), fade at the edges, and never sit under body text. A device is used in at least 3 places or it is decoration. [practice]
- Close with a side-by-side summary of all routes at the same size, in colour and grayscale.

## Presenting rules
1. Send the platform as a one-page recap first and confirm it is still right. It is what the work is judged against.
2. The final decision-maker attends. A stakeholder who sees a PDF later judges it without the reasoning.
3. Present live (call or in person). Never email logos without the conversation.
4. Running order per route: idea, mark large, mark small, touchpoints, rationale.
5. Equal finish across routes: an unpolished route reads as a decoy.
6. Never show a route you would not be proud to see chosen. Two good routes beat three with a filler.
7. Ask "which route best expresses [lead trait]?", not "which do you like?". State how to give feedback and when it is due.
8. 2 to 3 routes maximum: more looks like no point of view and invites mixing parts that do not belong together.

## Handling feedback
| Class | Meaning | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Fix | an error against the agreed platform or a measured gate | correct now, no round consumed |
| Change | preference within the agreed scope | counts as a revision round |
| New scope | new deliverable, new route, changed brief | quote separately before starting |
- Give the client a short form tied to the ranked traits and audience: "Which route best expresses [trait 1]?", "What feels wrong?" (problems, not solutions), "Where would this look out of place?"
- Suggest a day to reflect before decisions. Share files for internal discussion only after the presentation.
- "I don't like the colour": ask what is behind it. A personal association, a competitor resemblance and an audience mismatch need three different responses. Test the third with the audience, not the room.
- "Combine A's symbol with B's type": test it quickly and honestly; if it breaks the idea, show side by side why. Rebuild a coherent route and say what changed.
- 2 refinement rounds per phase (routes, then master files). A third signals an unclear brief: stop and re-confirm the platform. Say where the current round sits in every message.

## Mockups
- Method: use the client's real surfaces (their van model, storefront photo, uniform, real headlines and phone number, never lorem ipsum, their Business Profile on a phone) plus small-size cases (favicon, avatar circle, app icon).
- Mockups are drawn or composited. Label them "mockup" or "indicative"; never imply a photograph of a finished sign, vehicle or product exists when it is a drawing. Never present a stock scene as the client's real premises.
- Licensed mockup sources: check the licence for client presentations and commercial use before using a pack (LS Graphics, Shots.so, Apple Design Resources for device frames) `(checked Oct 2026, verify)`. Generic device frames are fine.
- Do not show every item on the same stock scene (a generic-work tell).

## Engagement scope and tiers
| Tier | Routes shown | Revision rounds | Guideline | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single asset | 1 | 2 | none | one asset on an approved brand, source files |
| 2-route lean | 2 | 3 | one-page brand sheet | logo suite, palette, type, primary touchpoint mockup |
| 3-route standard | 3 | per contract, 2 per phase | 10 to 20 page guidelines | the above plus device, 4 to 6 applications, file package |
| Full system | 3, territories first | per contract | full book and tokens | the above plus templates, imagery direction, rollout plan |
- Put in the contract: deliverables by name, routes, revision rounds per phase, what counts as new scope, response time owed by the client, kill fee.
- Deposit before work starts (about 50% is common) `[practice]`. Balance on delivery.
- IP: assignment of final rights on final payment; until then the client holds a presentation licence only. Rejected routes stay with the studio unless bought.
- Source release: master vector, outlined, and font licence list delivered on final payment. Fonts follow their licence (`typography.md`).
- White-label work carries the partner's brand or none (`guidelines.md`).

## Studio practice [practice]
- Design in black and white and as a silhouette first; add colour after the form works.
- Never show sketches or half-ideas; show only marks already tested on real applications.
- Run a trademark knockout search before presenting a finalist; a conflict found after the client falls in love is expensive.
- A symbol needs a reason (long name, sub-brands, shorthand, small spaces). Otherwise the name carries the identity and a good wordmark wins.
- Five-second test: show the mark for 5 seconds, hide it, ask what the business is, what they remember, how it feels. 5 to 10 people show patterns; small samples are noisy.
- Reference study: judge real work by named client, visible constraints, messy applications and independent coverage. Four failures out of ten means craft inspiration only. [limited]

## Editable template deliverables
- Build templates for the 3 to 6 assets the client's staff make most (social post, carousel, proposal cover, invoice, slide master, email signature) in the tool they use (Canva or Figma).
- Lock the logo layer and the type layer; leave only text, photo and one accent colour editable. Name layers and fonts by role. Set the brand colours and fonts in the tool's kit.
- Check each template at phone width and thumbnail size. Fix drift with a better template, not a stricter rule (`guidelines.md`).
- If the client's fonts are not in the tool, set the tool-safe fallback and say so.

## Personal-brand playbook [practice]
1. **Perception check:** ask 5 to 10 contacts, in writing, three words for the person and what they would be recommended for. Compare with the intended positioning.
2. **Peer feed audit:** capture 10 to 20 people in the same niche on the platform that matters; list shared colours, photo styles, formats; choose a signature colour and type that stand apart from the niche default (the beige serif coach look is now a category code).
3. **One photo session:** a written direction (background, light, wardrobe palette, crop, expression), 10 to 20 content frames plus avatar and banner. The face is the main asset.
4. **Template system:** name wordmark in one distinctive face, a monogram only for avatars, 3 to 5 fixed formats with constant type, colour, margins and name position.
5. Skip a symbol unless a company will grow from it or the name is long. Do not cite unsourced "consistency lifts revenue" figures to clients.

## Senior versus generic checklist
| Senior | Generic |
|---|---|
| platform written and confirmed before visuals | visuals first, strategy rationalised afterwards |
| routes differ in mechanism, mark type and type class | three palettes on one layout |
| every choice traced to a ranked trait | "it feels modern" |
| shows the hard cases (16 px, one colour, busy photo) | only flattering mockups |
| one device with rules, used 3 or more times | decoration added to look finished |
| measured gates stated with numbers | "should be accessible" |
| limits stated (trademark, print proofs, vector pass) | "final" with nothing verified |
| asks a specific question at the end | "let us know what you think" |
