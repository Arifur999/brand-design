# Supporting visual language

The mark is a small part of how a brand is recognised. Devices, imagery, type behaviour and layout habits carry it on every surface where the logo is small or absent.

## Graphic devices
- The most underused and often the most recognisable part of a small brand's identity. Derive 1 to 2 devices from the mark (a crop of the symbol, a letter's angle, a counter shape) or from the business (a road line for a courier, pool-tile grid for a pool builder, stitch line for a tailor). One generative shape can make patterns, dividers and containers.
- Each device needs rules: scale, crop ("may bleed off any edge, never cropped below half its width"), colours, placement, and never behind body text or across faces.
- Device library rules: patterns repeat at a fixed pitch (offset equals pitch so tiles join), fade or crop at edges deliberately, and each device appears in at least three places or it is decoration. Must work without the logo. **Logo-off test:** cover the logo on 5 assets; if you cannot tell they belong together, the constants are too weak. **Family test:** 10 assets side by side should look like siblings, not twins or strangers.

## Constants and variables
Write per element what never changes and what may: logo drawing constant, lockup variable · core hue and action role constant, secondary lead per campaign variable · type families and hierarchy constant, sizes within the scale variable · device geometry constant, crop and colour variable · imagery light, grade and honesty constant, subjects variable · voice traits constant, tone variable · grid, margins and logo position constant, composition variable.
Flex level: **tight** (franchises, healthcare, finance, many non-designers: templates for everything), **flexible** (hospitality, retail, professional services: locked constants plus a variable kit), **dynamic** (media, events, culture: a strong constant holding variable content). Decide by how many people make assets, on how many touchpoints, and how often the audience needs something new. Templates that fix placement, tint logic and type, and free one variable per instance (photo, colour), hold up best for non-designers. Define 2 to 3 signature compositions.

## Photography direction (write it as instructions a photographer can follow)
- **Subject:** what proves the main claim ("fast" = speed and arrival; "careful" = detail and hands). **Light:** soft daylight, high key, low key with one source, or hard flash. **Grade:** one preset for everything, stock included. **Composition:** eye level, low angle, flat lay, wide context or tight detail; leave a quiet area for type. **People:** candid action, direct portraits or hands only; real staff and customers with written releases, reflecting the actual customer base.
- Show context stock cannot fake (the technician outside a recognisable home). Retouch lightly. Never: handshakes, laptop-pointing, headset support agents, generic skylines, other companies' staff or vehicles, filters that change true colour.
- Real photography for local services, clinics, trades, restaurants and real estate; product UI, diagrams, illustration and real team photos for software and finance; illustration or non-identifying photos for sensitive subjects; renders labelled "indicative" for unbuilt places. Testimonials, team, before/after, portfolio and premises are only ever real, permissioned photos.
- Shot list: hero (landscape and vertical from the same setup), team portraits (same background, light and crop), action, details (tools, materials, hands), place (exterior with signage, interior, vehicles), results (before/after from the same spot and angle), customer moments. Phone shoots: clean lens, daylight, eye level, 1x lens, no beauty filters, several frames per setup in both orientations.
- Rights: a written licence covering web, social, ads and print with no end date; model and property releases. Unsplash and Pexels allow commercial use without credit but give no model-release guarantee: keep identifiable stock people out of endorsement contexts.
- **AI imagery:** acceptable for abstract textures, conceptual editorial art and moodboards marked as such. Never for staff, founders, customers, testimonial avatars, before/after, results, premises, vehicles, food or products presented as real (the US FTC rule on fake reviews, in force October 2024, covers testimonials from people who do not exist). Never as a final logo, symbol or icon.

## Illustration
Use when the subject cannot be photographed (a process, an abstract product, a sensitive topic). One style, ideally one illustrator; palette locked to the brand colours; line weight matched to the icon set; one perspective. "Photos prove, illustrations explain." Free sets (unDraw and similar) are fine for empty states but are not ownable as a signature. Isometric and 3D clay date fast; hand-drawn needs one hand.

## Iconography
One set, never mixed; stroke matches the adjacent text weight; corner style matches the radius language; sizes 16, 20, 24, 32; icons support labels, never replace them; no emoji as feature icons. A brand with a strong drawing style may need custom icons drawn on the mark's grid and stroke.

## Motion
3 to 5 principles, each tied to a trait ("calm: things fade and settle, nothing bounces"). Timing: small changes 100 to 150 ms, menus 200 ms, dialogs and sections 300 ms, large reveals 500 ms, exits a step faster. Animate transform and opacity; every pattern has a reduced-motion alternative. A logo animation lasts a few seconds and its end frame is identical to the static mark; storyboard it.

## Voice basics (verbal identity inside the guidelines)
- Voice is constant, tone shifts with context: errors calm and plain (no "Oops"), success warm and brief, bad news said first, legal plain with a summary on top, social the most relaxed version of the voice.
- Voice chart rows: trait · what it means · do · don't · example. "We sound like" a specific person, not an adjective.
- Find the voice in real language (reviews, the owner's emails, support replies); rewrite 3 real pieces (a hero line, an error message, a sales email) and test them.
- Headline tests: swap (replace the brand with a competitor: still works means not specific), so-what, proof on the same page, read aloud. Retire "Welcome to X", "your trusted partner", "[category] reimagined", "quality you can trust", "one-stop shop".
- Message hierarchy: one-sentence value proposition, three supporting pillars that are actually different from each other, each with proof, then objection answers in the customer's words, then 50 and 100 word boilerplates. Never invent numbers; use proof that needs none (a guarantee, a process, a named credential).

## Mascots, characters and partner badges
- A mascot needs a character sheet (poses, expressions, front, side and back views, colour, don'ts) and a head-only small version; never based on an existing character.
- Partner, payment, certification and award badges come only from the owner's official files with permission; until then use a labelled placeholder. Never redraw or recolour them.
