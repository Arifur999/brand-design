# Routes: cohesion, difference and defaults

Evidence tags: `[strong]` replicated research or a standard · `[limited]` few studies · `[practice]` designer consensus.

## One mechanism per route
- Write the mechanism before choosing anything: one sentence naming a single visual idea that yields the mark, the type and the palette together. Example: "a ledger's ruled line: hairline-cut wordmark, rule-thin symbol, ink-on-paper palette". One idea, three expressions. `[practice]`
- Sources for mechanisms that belong to this brand and not its category: a letterform in the name, a trade detail or tool, the place, a customer moment, the founder story, the product's structure. Build a word map first (20 to 40 words per branch: name, service, place, trade vocabulary, traits, story), cross-combine, strike clichés and anything a competitor uses.
- **One expression move:** exactly one loud component. Expressive mark: quiet type and palette. Expressive palette: plain mark. Expressive type: minimal graphics.
- Cohesion is consistency with the strategy, not "everything in one style"; art directors also look for an ownable element against competitors (Phillips et al. 2014). `[limited]`

## Personality axes (place every route on 2 to 4 of them, −2 to +2)
| Axis | Logo | Type | Colour |
|---|---|---|---|
| warm ↔ cool | round terminals, organic curves ↔ sharp, geometric | humanist ↔ grotesk, geometric | warm hue family and tinted neutrals ↔ cool |
| classic ↔ contemporary | monogram, serif, emblem ↔ abstract, wordmark | old-style serif ↔ neo-grotesk, variable | category code kept ↔ broken |
| playful ↔ serious | moving, asymmetric forms ↔ static, symmetric | elaborate, high x-height ↔ regular | high chroma, several hues ↔ low chroma, 1 to 2 hues |
| premium ↔ accessible | few parts, fine lines, space ↔ bold, filled | light weights, contrast serif ↔ bold sans | dark, few colours ↔ bright, several |
| quiet ↔ loud | small mark ↔ dominant mark | one family, narrow scale ↔ display + text, wide scale | neutral-led ↔ coloured surfaces |
| friend ↔ authority | hand-made ↔ institutional | rounded sans ↔ serif or grotesk | warm light ground ↔ dark saturated ground |

Axes adapt GV's brand-sprint sliders; real personality sits toward the ends. Logo dimensions (natural, harmonious, moderately elaborate; Henderson & Cote 1998) recur in type (Henderson et al. 2004; Grohmann et al. 2013). `[limited]` Scores are reasoned judgement.

## Making routes genuinely different
- Routes sit at different ends of at least two axes; three routes in the middle are one idea three times.
- Each component carries the difference: palette (hue family, or light versus dark structure), type (class, or clear width and weight difference), mark type (wordmark, lettermark, symbol plus wordmark, emblem). Differ in strategy, not hue.
- Inside a route, component positions stay within one step; a bigger gap is deliberate contrast with a written reason.
- Refresh: one axis becomes distance from the current identity (evolution to revolution).
- 2 to 3 routes, one recommended: too many complex options hurt choice when preferences are unclear (Chernev et al. 2015). `[strong]`
- At least one route without a symbol (wordmark or lettermark only) when the brief allows: models put a symbol plus wordmark in nearly every answer.

## The reflex test
For every font, colour and mark idea ask: "Would I propose this for another brand in this sector?" If yes, justify it from the brief (the person asked, the competitor map shows the region empty, an existing asset, a production constraint) or change it. At least one route uses nothing from the default list below. "Feels warm" or "feels premium" is not a justification; it is how the stereotype works.

## Model defaults to treat with suspicion
Measured in a public experiment (30 briefs, 180 answers across Claude models, Oct 2026; fatihaydost/brand-identity-skill) `[limited]`: the default is a **sector stereotype**; the same brief got the same display face 67 to 80% of the time.
1. Fraunces display and soft-serif neighbours (Recoleta): about a quarter of display picks.
2. Inter as the text face: 40% of text picks.
3. Cream ground + terracotta + gold or brass accent (+ serif display): the strongest cluster.
4. Navy + gold for law, property, finance, insurance, public sector.
5. Sector fonts: Cormorant Garamond (wine, hotel, property), Canela (law), Barlow Condensed (construction, logistics), Space Grotesk or Sora (AI, crypto, fintech), Poppins (edtech).
6. Indigo or violet in technology briefs (rare elsewhere, about 6% of primaries); blue-to-purple gradients are rare (about 5%) but read as a cliché when they appear.
7. Logo formula: the name's object + hidden letter + negative space + circle + a local "nod"; symbol + wordmark nearly always.

## Category clichés in human-made logos
Four-point spark, radial swirl, gradient orb (AI products); initial in a rounded square (that is the app-icon container, legitimate only as the app icon); hexagon, nodes and cube (tech); leaf, shield, roof chevron, arrow, bulb, globe, bolt; the swoosh; outline icon used as a logo; mockup polish over a weak form. Before building, name three clichés of this category. A cliché stays only if you can say who on the competitor shelf uses it, what second reading is this brand's own, and whether the idea survives without it. Sector specifics: `sectors.md`.

## Mixing routes
Clients often pick parts of different routes. Mix on request only: rebuild a route D, recolour D's mark to D's palette, re-place it on the axes, check cohesion, and say what had to change for the parts to fit. A kept component never changes.
